Lecture 20: Lecture 20: Cosmology - The early epoch

How does this lecture use Cosmology and Expansion, Scale Factor As Expansion History to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Solve evolution and global shape: The course then asks how whole spacetimes behave: how valid initial data evolves, how the universe expands, and how causal boundaries such as horizons are understood.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 20 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: The course needs to apply the field equation to a whole-universe model without pretending every detail of the universe is tracked. The course needs one changing quantity that records how large-scale distances evolve in a smoothed universe model. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is imposing homogeneity/isotropy, deriving reduced evolution equations, and reading matter content from how the scale factor changes. The operation is solving equations for this multiplier from matter content, curvature assumptions, and possible vacuum terms. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • A cosmological model uses symmetry assumptions plus a scale factor to reduce Einstein's equation to equations for large-scale evolution.
  • The scale factor is the time-dependent multiplier that converts fixed comoving separations into physical separations.
