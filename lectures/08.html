Lecture 08: Lecture 8: Parallel Transport & Curvature

How does this lecture use Connections and Parallel Transport, Curvature, Geodesics As Free Motion, Parallel Transport Loop Test, Riemann Curvature As Noncommuting Change to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Turn falling into geometry: The course must explain free motion without saying that every path is judged against an invisible flat background. It needs its own rule for straightness and its own test for real gravitational curvature.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 08 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: To compare directions at different points, the course needs a rule for carrying a direction along a path. The theory needs a local test for whether transported directions come back changed after moving around a tiny loop. The course needs a precise replacement for straight-line motion when spacetime may be curved. The course needs an operational test for curvature that does not rely on seeing a surface bend. The course needs a compact way to record how two small changes in different orders fail to agree. The mathematical work to watch is: The central operation is covariant differentiation: measuring how a field changes after subtracting the change caused merely by moving through tangent spaces. The operation is taking a commutator of covariant derivatives or comparing nearby geodesics to measure relative acceleration. The operation is checking whether the curve's tangent has zero covariant change along the curve. The operation is parallel transport around a small loop and reading the mismatch as evidence of curvature. The operation is comparing the two orders of corrected differentiation and measuring the leftover mismatch. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • A connection is a rule for differentiating vector fields along directions and for transporting vectors from one tangent space to another.
  • Curvature is the tensorial failure of covariant derivatives to commute, equivalently the failure of parallel transport around small loops to return a vector unchanged.
  • A geodesic is a curve whose tangent direction is transported along itself according to the connection.
  • The loop test carries a vector around a closed path using the connection and compares the returned vector with the starting one.
  • The Riemann curvature tensor records the failure of two covariant derivatives, taken in different orders, to give the same result.
