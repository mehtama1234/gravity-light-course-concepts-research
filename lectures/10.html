Lecture 10: Lecture 10: Metric Manifolds

How does this lecture use Metric Manifolds, Metric Signature And Light Cones, Proper Time As Clock Reading to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Turn falling into geometry: The course must explain free motion without saying that every path is judged against an invisible flat background. It needs its own rule for straightness and its own test for real gravitational curvature.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 10 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: After building nearness and smoothness, the course needs a rule that tells clocks, rulers, and light cones what they measure. The course needs a measurement rule that separates possible time-like motion, space-like separation, and light-like travel. The course needs a way to say what a clock actually records along its own path through spacetime. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is evaluating the metric on tangent vectors to compute intervals, norms, angles, volumes, and null directions. The operation is evaluating the metric on tangent directions and sorting them into time-like, space-like, or null. The operation is evaluating the metric on the path's tangent and adding the resulting clock increments along the path. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • A metric is a smoothly varying inner product on tangent spaces, with Lorentzian signature in spacetime so that time and space enter differently.
  • Lorentzian signature is the metric's time-space sign pattern, and light cones are the resulting boundaries of causal influence.
  • Proper time is the time measured along a time-like path by integrating the metric interval along that path.
