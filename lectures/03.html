Lecture 03: Lecture 3: Multilinear Algebra

How does this lecture use Multilinear Algebra, Dual Vectors Measure Directions, Tensor Components Versus The Object to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Build space before measuring it: A theory of gravity cannot begin by assuming a fixed grid, fixed rulers, or a surrounding room. These lectures build the minimum language needed to speak about places, change, and physical quantities before distance has been granted.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 03 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: Physics needs quantities that accept directions, measurements, and flows while keeping track of how many slots they have. The course needs a way to distinguish an arrow-like direction from a measuring rule applied to that direction. The course needs students to separate a tensor itself from the list of numbers used to display it in one basis. The mathematical work to watch is: The key operation is feeding vectors and covectors into slots, changing basis, and contracting matching slots to produce coordinate-independent quantities. The operation is feeding a vector into the covector and getting a number that changes correctly when coordinates change. The operation is transforming components under a basis change while preserving the tensor's action on its input slots. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • A tensor is a multilinear rule with specified input slots; it returns a number or another structured object in a way compatible with linear combinations.
  • A dual vector, also called a covector or one-form, is a linear measuring rule on vectors.
  • The tensor is the coordinate-independent multilinear rule; components are the rule's numbers after choosing basis vectors and dual basis covectors.
