Lecture 21: Lecture 21: Cosmology - The late epoch

How does this lecture use Cosmology and Expansion, Cosmological Constant As Vacuum Term, Redshift As Stretched Light, Scale Factor As Expansion History, Hubble Rate As Change Of Scale to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Solve evolution and global shape: The course then asks how whole spacetimes behave: how valid initial data evolves, how the universe expands, and how causal boundaries such as horizons are understood.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 21 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: The course needs to apply the field equation to a whole-universe model without pretending every detail of the universe is tracked. The course needs room for a uniform term in the field equation that behaves like spacetime itself carries energy. The course needs to explain how geometry changes the light that finally reaches an observer. The course needs one changing quantity that records how large-scale distances evolve in a smoothed universe model. The course needs a way to measure how quickly cosmic scale is changing at a given time. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is imposing homogeneity/isotropy, deriving reduced evolution equations, and reading matter content from how the scale factor changes. The operation is adding this term to the field equation and studying how it changes expansion without clumping like matter. The operation is comparing the light's wave direction with the emitter's and observer's time directions. The operation is solving equations for this multiplier from matter content, curvature assumptions, and possible vacuum terms. The operation is comparing how fast the cosmic scale changes to the current size of that scale. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • A cosmological model uses symmetry assumptions plus a scale factor to reduce Einstein's equation to equations for large-scale evolution.
  • The cosmological constant is a constant term in Einstein's equation, often read as a vacuum energy contribution.
  • Redshift is the change in measured light frequency between emission and observation along a null path.
  • The scale factor is the time-dependent multiplier that converts fixed comoving separations into physical separations.
  • The Hubble rate is the rate of change of the scale factor divided by the scale factor itself.
