Lecture 14: Lecture 14: Matter

How does this lecture use Matter and Stress-Energy to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Make laws coordinate-free: Once geometry exists, the course needs laws that can be stated without picking a favorite observer or map. It also needs a precise object for what matter contributes to gravity.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 14 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: Gravity needs a precise account of what matter contributes: not just mass, but energy, momentum, pressure, and stresses. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is evaluating its slots against observer directions and surface normals, then requiring local conservation through covariant divergence. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Starting Problem

This lecture starts from a concrete obstruction: Gravity needs a precise account of what matter contributes: not just mass, but energy, momentum, pressure, and stresses. It should not be read as a lecture about terminology. The tempting starting picture is: A Newtonian habit says gravity is sourced by mass alone, with pressure and momentum treated as secondary details. The lecture uses Matter and Stress-Energy to replace that picture with a more careful construction. The reader's job is to notice what has to be built before the later gravity statement is allowed: a kind of nearness, a local naming rule, a slot-aware object, a comparison rule, a measurement rule, or a source accounting rule. The point is to make the hidden assumption visible before any formal expression is trusted.

Mathematical Turn

The mathematical turn is to choose the right object and then perform the operation that tests it. For this lecture the first object is: The stress-energy tensor is the object that measures energy density, momentum flow, pressure, and stress through chosen directions or surfaces. The first operation is: The operation is evaluating its slots against observer directions and surface normals, then requiring local conservation through covariant divergence. The next linked idea, Matter and Stress-Energy, keeps the same pressure by asking whether the statement survives a change of chart, basis, observer, path, or local test. By the time the lecture reaches Matter and Stress-Energy, the reader should be able to say what is being acted on, what operation is legal, and what would break if the operation depended on a preferred drawing.

Worked Path Through The Lecture

  1. Begin with the everyday problem: Gravity needs a precise account of what matter contributes: not just mass, but energy, momentum, pressure, and stresses.
  2. Reject the naive picture: Relativity treats energy and momentum as a unified local bookkeeping object, and pressure/stress also gravitate.
  3. Name the object that carries the idea: The stress-energy tensor is the object that measures energy density, momentum flow, pressure, and stress through chosen directions or surfaces.
  4. Perform the operation that makes the idea usable: The operation is evaluating its slots against observer directions and surface normals, then requiring local conservation through covariant divergence.
  5. Connect it forward through Matter and Stress-Energy: Einstein gravity relates spacetime curvature to this full matter bookkeeping, which is why light, fields, and pressure affect gravity.
