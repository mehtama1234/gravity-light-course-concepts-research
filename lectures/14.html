Lecture 14: Lecture 14: Matter

How does this lecture use Matter and Stress-Energy to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Make laws coordinate-free: Once geometry exists, the course needs laws that can be stated without picking a favorite observer or map. It also needs a precise object for what matter contributes to gravity.

First-Principles Role

This lecture prepares the matter side of gravity. Stress-energy records energy, momentum flow, pressure, and stress in a form that can be locally conserved and later balanced with geometry.

Starting Problem

Matter cannot enter Einstein gravity as a vague pile of stuff. Relativity needs to know energy density, momentum flow, pressure, and stress, because all of those contribute to the gravitational source. A simple mass-only picture is too thin. This lecture teaches matter as local accounting: what an observer measures, what crosses a surface, and how that bookkeeping changes from point to point. Only after that can matter sit on the right side of the field equation.

Mathematical Turn

The mathematical turn is to use the stress-energy tensor as the matter bookkeeping object. Its slots let observers and surface directions ask precise questions about energy and momentum. Local conservation is stated using covariant divergence, because the accounting must respect curved spacetime comparison. This is the source-side preparation for Einstein's equation: matter must be written as a tensor object with conservation behavior that can match the geometry side.

Worked Path Through The Lecture

  1. Start by asking what an observer at an event can measure about matter.
  2. Use stress-energy slots to read energy density, momentum flow, pressure, or stress.
  3. Require the matter accounting to obey local covariant conservation in curved spacetime.
  4. Prepare that tensor object to balance the geometry side of Einstein's equation.

Objects To Track

  • The stress-energy tensor is the object that measures energy density, momentum flow, pressure, and stress through chosen directions or surfaces.

Linked Concepts

Matter and Stress-Energy

Gravity bends space and time. But what causes the bending? Newton said: mass. Einstein's answer is richer. Mass does bend spacetime—but so does energy, momentum (motion), pressure (crowding), and internal stress. Relativity treats all of these as one unified object, because energy and momentum are really the same thing seen from different angles. Gravity must respond to all of them together.

Object: A stress-energy tensor is a 4×4 bookkeeping table (like a spreadsheet with 4 rows and 4 columns). Each cell holds one number that describes a 'flow': a kind of stuff moving in a direction. Top-left corner: energy density (how much energy per unit volume, measured by someone at rest). Top-right block (three entries): momentum flowing—the push and tug through space. Bottom-right 3×3 block: pressure and internal stress—how much matter inside one region pushes on neighboring regions. The row tells you 'what stuff are we counting' (energy or momentum component). The column tells you 'in which direction is it flowing.' Multiply the numbers by a direction vector (an arrow showing which way you are looking) and you get a measurable quantity in that direction.

Operation: Take the tensor (the 4×4 table). Point an arrow in some direction—say, the direction an observer is moving, or the outward normal to a surface. Multiply each row by components of that arrow and sum. The result: the energy density that observer measures, or the momentum flux passing through that surface, or stress between regions. Different arrows give different answers, but all observers agree on the physical truth underneath.
