Lecture 23: Lecture 23: Penrose Diagrams

How does this lecture use Black Holes and Horizons, Event Horizon As Causal Boundary, Penrose Diagrams Compress Infinity to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Solve evolution and global shape: The course then asks how whole spacetimes behave: how valid initial data evolves, how the universe expands, and how causal boundaries such as horizons are understood.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 23 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: The course needs to understand regions where causal structure, not escape speed folklore, defines what can communicate outward. The course needs a definition of a black-hole boundary that depends on what signals can do, not on a material surface. The course needs a way to draw causal structure, including infinity, without losing which light paths are possible. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is tracing null directions and causal relations, often after a coordinate change that removes fake singular behavior. The operation is tracing future-directed null paths and deciding which events can communicate outward. The operation is conformally rescaling the metric so causal directions remain visible while distances are compressed. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • An event horizon is a boundary in spacetime's causal structure; Penrose diagrams compress infinities to show which events can influence which others.
  • An event horizon is the boundary separating events that can send light to distant outside observers from events that cannot.
  • A Penrose diagram is a rescaled picture that preserves light-cone directions while bringing infinity to a finite edge.
