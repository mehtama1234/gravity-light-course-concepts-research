Initial Data Must Satisfy Constraints

The course needs to explain why not every imagined snapshot of space can be the start of a valid spacetime.

Naive Picture

The ordinary mechanics habit says choose positions and velocities freely, then evolve them.

Why It Fails

That fails in general relativity because the geometry of one slice and its change must already satisfy constraint equations.

Mathematical Object

Initial data consists of spatial geometry plus momentum-like change data that obeys Hamiltonian and momentum constraints.

Operation

The operation is checking the constraint equations before using the data as a lawful starting point.

Why It Matters

This matters for numerical gravity, black-hole mergers, and wave prediction, where bad initial data creates fake physics.

What Breaks Without It

Without constraints, a calculation can evolve a snapshot that no solution of Einstein's equation could contain.

Mini Example

A bridge blueprint cannot choose every beam angle independently; the pieces must already fit before construction starts.

Common Confusion

Constraints are not optional extra equations. They decide whether the starting slice belongs to any spacetime solution.

Evening Lecture B: Quantum versus Gravity

Pressure test: Put classical geometry and quantum matter on opposite sides of the same proposed equation and ask what kind of object each side is. The evening-lecture pressure is to notice that a definite spacetime source is no longer straightforward when matter is treated quantum mechanically.

Why it changes this concept: This makes quantum gravity a bookkeeping crisis, not a fashionable topic. The concept pages on constraints, source terms, and canonical formulation now point toward the place where classical spacetime stops being obviously self-contained. The reader sees why a source term is easy to write classically and hard to justify quantum mechanically.

The selected span introduces semiclassical Einstein equations, putting Einstein geometry on one side and a quantum-matter expectation value on the other. That span exposes the central tension in ordinary terms: the left side wants a definite geometry, while the right side comes from quantum bookkeeping. The page reads the lecture as a pressure test for the course's source and constraint machinery.

Lecture 18: Lecture 18: Canonical Formulation of GR I

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sOiifkFYck4&t=1542s

The lecturer uses the contracted Bianchi identity to explain why the time-time and time-space parts of Einstein's equations are not evolution equations. They contain only the needed lower time derivatives, so they restrict the starting fields instead of advancing them. He says four of the ten Einstein equations are constraints on initial data and six are evolution equations.
