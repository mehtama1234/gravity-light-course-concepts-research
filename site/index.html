Lecture Families

Build space before measuring it

Problem: A theory of gravity cannot begin by assuming a fixed grid, fixed rulers, or a surrounding room. These lectures build the minimum language needed to speak about places, change, and physical quantities before distance has been granted.

Topology supplies nearness. Manifolds supply local coordinate patches. Multilinear algebra supplies slot-aware quantities. Tangent spaces and fields supply local directions and point-by-point variation.

Transcript-backed for every lecture in this family.

Turn falling into geometry

Problem: The course must explain free motion without saying that every path is judged against an invisible flat background. It needs its own rule for straightness and its own test for real gravitational curvature.

Connections define how directions are compared along paths. Parallel transport applies that comparison. Curvature measures the failure of those comparisons to fit around loops. The metric later says what clocks, rulers, and light cones measure.

Transcript-backed except listed gaps.

Make laws coordinate-free

Problem: Once geometry exists, the course needs laws that can be stated without picking a favorite observer or map. It also needs a precise object for what matter contributes to gravity.

Symmetry identifies transformations that preserve structure. Integration adds local geometric quantities over regions. Spacetime supplies the Lorentzian stage. Stress-energy states what matter carries. Einstein's equation couples the geometry side to the matter side.

Transcript-backed for every lecture in this family.

Read spacetime with light

Problem: The course needs a bridge from equations to observation. Light is the bridge because it follows the causal structure set by the metric and carries information to detectors.

Null directions are tangent directions whose spacetime interval is zero. Null geodesics are light paths. Families of such paths focus, shear, delay, or bend according to the geometry.

Transcript-backed for every lecture in this family.

Solve evolution and global shape

Problem: The course then asks how whole spacetimes behave: how valid initial data evolves, how the universe expands, and how causal boundaries such as horizons are understood.

Canonical GR rewrites spacetime as slice data plus constraints. Cosmology uses symmetry to reduce the field equation. Black holes and Penrose diagrams track causal reach rather than visual shape.

Transcript-backed except listed gaps.

Extract small signals

Problem: Exact solutions are rare, but real detectors measure small changes in realistic systems. The course needs controlled approximations that separate physical effects from coordinate changes and connect source motion to measurable detector strain.

Perturbation theory studies small metric changes around a known background. Quantizable matter raises how matter fields source gravity. Wave-source and detector lectures connect those changes to strain in interferometers.

Transcript-backed except listed gaps.

Course Spine

Foundations

Question: What must be built before gravity can be described without a preferred coordinate grid?

The early lectures strip geometry down to nearness, local charts, slot-aware algebra, and local directions. This prevents later equations from smuggling in a flat background.

These ideas prevent later gravity from depending on a preferred picture, grid, or surrounding flat space.

Geometry of Gravity

Question: How does the course turn falling into a statement about geometry?

Connections define straight motion on a manifold, curvature measures when those local straightness rules fail to fit globally, and the metric turns measurement into a field.

These ideas turn the felt fact of falling into precise comparisons of local directions and measurements.

Dynamics and Matter

Question: What does the field equation actually join together?

It joins a divergence-free curvature object to stress-energy, so local conservation of matter and local geometry obey the same accounting rule.

These ideas explain why the field equation is a constrained balance between geometry and matter, not a slogan.

Light and Observation

Question: How does abstract spacetime become something seen by detectors?

Null geodesics, lensing, wave perturbations, and interferometer strain translate metric structure into observed light paths and measured relative lengths.

These ideas explain how spacetime geometry becomes observations through light paths and detector response.

Applications

Question: Where do the foundations get stress-tested?

Cosmology, black holes, and wave sources force local geometry, global causal structure, matter, and measurement to operate as one system.

These ideas show why the machinery was built: it handles universe-scale evolution, horizons, and waves.
