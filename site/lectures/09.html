Lecture 09: Lecture 9: Newtonian spacetime is curved!

How does this lecture use Curvature, Riemann Curvature As Noncommuting Change, Ricci Curvature As Volume Change to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Turn falling into geometry: The course must explain free motion without saying that every path is judged against an invisible flat background. It needs its own rule for straightness and its own test for real gravitational curvature.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 09 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: The theory needs a local test for whether transported directions come back changed after moving around a tiny loop. The course needs a compact way to record how two small changes in different orders fail to agree. The course needs the part of curvature that measures how a small bundle of freely moving paths changes size. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is taking a commutator of covariant derivatives or comparing nearby geodesics to measure relative acceleration. The operation is comparing the two orders of corrected differentiation and measuring the leftover mismatch. The operation is contracting slots of the Riemann tensor to keep the part relevant for volume change. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Starting Problem

This lecture starts from a concrete obstruction: The theory needs a local test for whether transported directions come back changed after moving around a tiny loop. It should not be read as a lecture about terminology. The tempting starting picture is: Curvature is often pictured only as visible bending in a drawing, as though an outside viewpoint were part of the theory. The lecture uses Curvature, Riemann Curvature As Noncommuting Change, Ricci Curvature As Volume Change to replace that picture with a more careful construction. The reader's job is to notice what has to be built before the later gravity statement is allowed: a kind of nearness, a local naming rule, a slot-aware object, a comparison rule, a measurement rule, or a source accounting rule. The point is to make the hidden assumption visible before any formal expression is trusted.

Mathematical Turn

The mathematical turn is to choose the right object and then perform the operation that tests it. For this lecture the first object is: Curvature is the tensorial failure of covariant derivatives to commute, equivalently the failure of parallel transport around small loops to return a vector unchanged. The first operation is: The operation is taking a commutator of covariant derivatives or comparing nearby geodesics to measure relative acceleration. The next linked idea, Riemann Curvature As Noncommuting Change, keeps the same pressure by asking whether the statement survives a change of chart, basis, observer, path, or local test. By the time the lecture reaches Ricci Curvature As Volume Change, the reader should be able to say what is being acted on, what operation is legal, and what would break if the operation depended on a preferred drawing.

Worked Path Through The Lecture

  1. Begin with the everyday problem: The theory needs a local test for whether transported directions come back changed after moving around a tiny loop.
  2. Reject the naive picture: Spacetime curvature can be present without any outside visual bend. The intrinsic test is whether transport around loops and nearby free-fall paths fail to agree.
  3. Name the object that carries the idea: Curvature is the tensorial failure of covariant derivatives to commute, equivalently the failure of parallel transport around small loops to return a vector unchanged.
  4. Perform the operation that makes the idea usable: The operation is taking a commutator of covariant derivatives or comparing nearby geodesics to measure relative acceleration.
  5. Connect it forward through Ricci Curvature As Volume Change: Einstein gravity uses Ricci-type curvature to connect geometry with stress-energy in the field equation.

Objects To Track

  • Curvature is the tensorial failure of covariant derivatives to commute, equivalently the failure of parallel transport around small loops to return a vector unchanged.
  • The Riemann curvature tensor records the failure of two covariant derivatives, taken in different orders, to give the same result.
  • Ricci curvature is a contraction of the full curvature tensor that captures focusing or spreading of nearby geodesic families.
