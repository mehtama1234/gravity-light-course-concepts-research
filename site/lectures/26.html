Lecture 26: Lecture 26: How quantizable matter gravitates

How does this lecture use Matter and Stress-Energy to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Extract small signals: Exact solutions are rare, but real detectors measure small changes in realistic systems. The course needs controlled approximations that separate physical effects from coordinate changes and connect source motion to measurable detector strain.

First-Principles Role

This lecture exposes the source-side tension between classical geometry and quantized matter. It asks what stress-energy can mean when matter is no longer a definite classical field.

Starting Problem

Quantizable matter raises a pressure point in the course's classical story. Einstein gravity wants a definite stress-energy source for a definite spacetime geometry. Quantum matter does not fit that picture as simply as a classical fluid or field. The lecture is not about adding fashionable quantum language. It asks what it means for matter to gravitate when the matter description no longer behaves like one ordinary local source assignment.

Mathematical Turn

The mathematical turn is to revisit stress-energy as the source object and ask what kind of value it can have when matter is treated quantum mechanically. Classically, the tensor records local energy, momentum, pressure, and stress. In a quantum setting, expectation values, fluctuations, and operator meaning complicate the source side. The issue is a bookkeeping mismatch: the geometry side remains classical while the matter side resists being one definite classical tensor.

Worked Path Through The Lecture

  1. Start with classical stress-energy as the source side of Einstein's equation.
  2. Replace classical matter with quantized matter and ask what source value is available.
  3. Notice that expectation, fluctuation, and locality questions change the source bookkeeping.
  4. Read the tension as a reason quantum gravity is a structural problem, not a branding problem.

Objects To Track

  • The stress-energy tensor is the object that measures energy density, momentum flow, pressure, and stress through chosen directions or surfaces.

Linked Concepts

Matter and Stress-Energy

Gravity must account for what makes matter curve spacetime: not just mass, but energy, momentum, pressure, and internal stresses all push back on geometry. Newton thought mass was enough, but relativity reveals energy and momentum are faces of one unified object.

Object: The stress-energy tensor is a 4×4 table where each entry records a flow: energy flowing in time (top-left), momentum flowing through space (top-right block), pressure and stress between spatial directions (bottom-right 3×3 block). Rows ask 'what kind of stuff,' columns ask 'flowing which direction.'

Operation: Multiply the tensor entries by direction vectors representing an observer or surface normal. The result tells you the energy density that observer measures, the momentum flux through a surface, or the internal stress between regions.
