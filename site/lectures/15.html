Lecture 15: Lecture 15: Einstein Gravity

How does this lecture use Matter and Stress-Energy, Einstein Equation, Ricci Curvature As Volume Change, Einstein Tensor As Conserved Geometry, Cosmological Constant As Vacuum Term to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Make laws coordinate-free: Once geometry exists, the course needs laws that can be stated without picking a favorite observer or map. It also needs a precise object for what matter contributes to gravity.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 15 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: Gravity needs a precise account of what matter contributes: not just mass, but energy, momentum, pressure, and stresses. The course needs the law that says how spacetime geometry and matter constrain each other. The course needs the part of curvature that measures how a small bundle of freely moving paths changes size. The course needs the geometry side of the field equation to have the same local conservation behavior as matter. The course needs room for a uniform term in the field equation that behaves like spacetime itself carries energy. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is evaluating its slots against observer directions and surface normals, then requiring local conservation through covariant divergence. The operation is equating the geometric tensor to stress-energy, then solving the resulting coupled nonlinear field equations under boundary or symmetry assumptions. The operation is contracting slots of the Riemann tensor to keep the part relevant for volume change. The operation is combining Ricci curvature, scalar curvature, and the metric so the result has the required conservation property. The operation is adding this term to the field equation and studying how it changes expansion without clumping like matter. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • The stress-energy tensor is the object that measures energy density, momentum flow, pressure, and stress through chosen directions or surfaces.
  • The Einstein tensor is built from curvature and the metric so that its covariant divergence vanishes, matching local conservation of stress-energy.
  • Ricci curvature is a contraction of the full curvature tensor that captures focusing or spreading of nearby geodesic families.
  • The Einstein tensor is the curvature combination whose covariant divergence vanishes automatically.
  • The cosmological constant is a constant term in Einstein's equation, often read as a vacuum energy contribution.
