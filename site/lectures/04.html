Lecture 04: Lecture 4: Differentiable Manifolds

How does this lecture use Manifolds and Local Flatness, Charts, Atlases, and Overlap Rules, Smoothness As Allowed Change to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Build space before measuring it: A theory of gravity cannot begin by assuming a fixed grid, fixed rulers, or a surrounding room. These lectures build the minimum language needed to speak about places, change, and physical quantities before distance has been granted.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 04 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: The course needs spaces that can be curved overall while still looking ordinary to a small local observer. The course needs many local maps that can cover one space without pretending one map works everywhere. The course needs to know when change is regular enough for derivatives to make sense across chart boundaries. The mathematical work to watch is: The main operation is changing charts and checking that statements survive the change rather than depending on one chosen coordinate grid. The operation is comparing charts on their overlaps and requiring the change from one chart to another to behave smoothly. The operation is taking derivatives in one chart and checking that another chart gives the same kind of admissible calculation. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • A manifold is a space covered by charts, each chart translating a small patch into ordinary numbers while overlap maps say when two descriptions agree.
  • A chart assigns numbers to points in one patch, and an atlas is a compatible collection of such charts.
  • A smooth structure is the rule that says which chart changes are differentiable enough for calculus to be done consistently.
