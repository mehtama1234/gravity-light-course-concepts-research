Lecture 17: Lecture 17: Optical Geometry II

How does this lecture use Optical Geometry and Null Geodesics, Gravitational Lensing And Focusing to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Read spacetime with light: The course needs a bridge from equations to observation. Light is the bridge because it follows the causal structure set by the metric and carries information to detectors.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 17 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: The course needs to explain why light follows paths fixed by spacetime geometry rather than by an external optical medium. The course needs to explain why masses can change apparent positions and brightness of distant sources. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is solving the geodesic equation under the null condition and comparing neighboring light rays through focusing or lensing. The operation is tracking neighboring light paths and measuring how the bundle changes direction, area, or shape. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • Null geodesics are curves whose tangent has zero metric length and is parallel transported along itself.
  • Lensing is the bending and focusing of families of null geodesics by spacetime curvature.
