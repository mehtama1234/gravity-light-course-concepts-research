Lecture 28: Lecture 28: How to detect gravitational waves

How does this lecture use Perturbation Theory and Gravitational Waves, Strain As Relative Length Change, Interferometer Light As A Ruler to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Extract small signals: Exact solutions are rare, but real detectors measure small changes in realistic systems. The course needs controlled approximations that separate physical effects from coordinate changes and connect source motion to measurable detector strain.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 28 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: The course needs a way to study small departures from a known spacetime and connect them to measurable signals. The course needs to say what a gravitational-wave detector actually measures in ordinary terms. The course needs to connect gravitational-wave geometry to the instrument that reads it. The mathematical work to watch is: The operation is linearizing the field equations, separating physical modes from coordinate changes, and predicting detector strain. The operation is comparing two perpendicular arm lengths over time and reading the changing difference. The operation is recombining the light and measuring changes in interference caused by different travel times along the arms. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

  • Strain is fractional length change: change in separation divided by the original separation.
  • An interferometer uses split laser light traveling along different arms as an extremely sensitive ruler.
