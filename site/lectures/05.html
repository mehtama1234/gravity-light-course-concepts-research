Lecture 05: Lecture 5: Tangent Spaces

How does this lecture use Tangent Spaces and Fields, Dual Vectors Measure Directions, Tangent Vectors As Derivatives to move the course from everyday geometric intuition toward a usable theory of gravity and light?

Family Role

Build space before measuring it: A theory of gravity cannot begin by assuming a fixed grid, fixed rulers, or a surrounding room. These lectures build the minimum language needed to speak about places, change, and physical quantities before distance has been granted.

First-Principles Role

Lecture 05 should be read as a step in the course's construction, not as an isolated topic. The ordinary problem is: The course needs a way to attach possible directions of motion to every event, then let physical quantities vary from event to event. The course needs a way to distinguish an arrow-like direction from a measuring rule applied to that direction. The course needs an internal definition of a direction at a point without drawing an arrow in a larger space. The mathematical work to watch is: The basic operation is differentiating a function along a tangent direction and then doing this smoothly point by point across the manifold. The operation is feeding a vector into the covector and getting a number that changes correctly when coordinates change. The operation is applying the tangent vector to a function and reading the directional rate of change. A good writeup should make that operation visible before using later terms from relativity.

Objects To Track

  • A tangent space is the vector space of directions available at one point; a field assigns a value, such as a vector or tensor, to each point.
  • A dual vector, also called a covector or one-form, is a linear measuring rule on vectors.
  • A tangent vector can be understood as a rule that tells how functions change at a point along a possible direction.
