Tutorial 13: Schwarzschild Spacetime

The tutorial practices reading a black-hole spacetime through geometry and causal reach.

Mathematical Object

The Schwarzschild metric, radial coordinates, horizons, and causal directions.

Operation

Track which coordinate features are removable and which causal boundaries remain after better descriptions are chosen.

Why It Matters

Black holes test whether metric, null paths, and coordinates have been separated cleanly.

What Breaks Without It

Without this practice, a coordinate singularity can be mistaken for a physical surface.

First-Principles Role

This tutorial uses Schwarzschild spacetime to test whether students can separate geometry from coordinates. The ordinary problem is that a formula can look singular even when the spacetime fact being described is not the same kind of singularity. The tutorial asks students to slow down and decide whether a bad-looking expression marks a true geometric obstruction or only a poor way of naming events.

Mathematical Detail In Plain Language

The object is the Schwarzschild metric and the causal structure it defines. The operation is to examine radial behavior, horizon behavior, and coordinate changes while keeping null directions and physical invariants in view. The horizon is not treated as a material wall; it is read through what future-directed light can do.

How It Connects

This tutorial prepares the black-hole and Penrose-diagram lectures. It forces the earlier manifold and metric lessons to work together: coordinates are useful displays, but causal reach is the physical question. That is why the horizon cannot be understood by staring only at one metric component; one must track allowed light directions and whether signals can reach outside observers.

Source Span Read

The chosen span begins with geodesics in Schwarzschild spacetime on a manifold equipped with the Schwarzschild metric. That pins the tutorial to motion read from geometry, not from an external force picture. The page therefore stresses the difference between a coordinate display and the causal paths the metric actually permits.
