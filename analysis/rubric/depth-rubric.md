# Depth Rubric

This project treats a lecture as a mathematical argument, not as a bag of terms.

## Evidence Records

Each evidence record must include:

- the lecture title and local transcript path;
- a timestamp when a VTT cue is available;
- the local transcript snippet;
- the lecture argument in plain words;
- the mathematical object being introduced;
- the operation performed on that object;
- the reason the object or operation matters later in the course;
- a caveat, limitation, or common confusion.

## Concept Pages

A concept page is too shallow if it only defines a term. It must include:

- the naive picture;
- why the naive picture fails;
- the first-principles object;
- the operation;
- a worked mini-example;
- where it appears in the lecture sequence;
- what breaks without it;
- transcript-backed evidence.

## Theme Pages

Themes must explain a throughline across lectures. For example, "curvature" must
connect topology, tangent spaces, connections, parallel transport, metric geometry,
Einstein gravity, black holes, and gravitational waves.

## Integrated Companion Layer

The archive is too shallow if tutorials and evening lectures are only extra pages.
The companion layer must include:

- tutorial or evening pressure tests on every concept supported by archive videos;
- a cross-video learning path based on what the reader must be able to do;
- a dependency map that states what breaks if a mathematical idea is skipped;
- a mathematical-why companion that explains the shared mathematical moves with worked examples;
- family and theme pages that surface tutorial pressure, not just lecture groups;
- validation that checks these artifacts render and remain linked.
